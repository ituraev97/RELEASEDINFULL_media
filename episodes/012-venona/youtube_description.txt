How did America catch Stalin's atomic spies? Partly by reading their mail. VENONA was the U.S. Army's secret project to break the KGB's wartime cables, and the declassified NSA translations show how codebreakers at Arlington Hall exposed Soviet spies inside the Manhattan Project: Klaus Fuchs, Harry Gold, David Greenglass and Julius and Ethel Rosenberg.
We read the cables themselves, including the November 1944 telegram about Theodore Hall, a 19-year-old physicist at Los Alamos who was questioned by the FBI and never prosecuted. Along the way: Gene Grabeel and Meredith Gardner, the reused one-time pads, the CIA's estimate for the first Soviet bomb, the 1949 test, the atom spy trials, and the 1995 release of the VENONA files.

Facts come from documents and official sources. Claims are attributed. Theories are labelled as theories.
