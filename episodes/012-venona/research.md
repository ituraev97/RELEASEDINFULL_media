### A. Primary documents (NSA VENONA translations)
- New York → Moscow No. 1585, 12 Nov 1944, "Decision to maintain contact with Theodore Hall (1944)", issued 25/4/1961: https://archive.org/details/12nov_possible_new_recruit — "BEK visited Theodore HALL, 19 years old, the son of a furrier. He is a graduate of HARVARD University. As a talented physicist he was taken on for government work… According to BEK's account HALL has an exceptionally keen mind and a broad outlook, and is politically developed. At the present time H. is in charge of a group at "CAMP-2" (SANTA-FE). H. handed over to BEK a report about the CAMP and named the key personnel employed on ENORMOUS… H. left his photograph and came to an understanding with BEK about a place for meeting him." Comments: CAMP-2: LOS ALAMOS; ENORMOUS: Manhattan Engineering District – U.S. Atomic Energy Project; BEK: S. N. Kurnakov; VIKTOR: Lt. Gen. P. M. Fitin.

### B. Official histories
- U.S. Army ICoE (E. Thompson), "SIS Uncovers Soviet Infiltration of Manhattan Project (20 DEC 1946)", DVIDS 2024: https://www.dvidshub.net/news/487461 — VENONA established Feb 1943; Grabeel & Zubko first; five systems; Mundy quote on code book/additives/one-time pad, "widely considered unbreakable"; Hallock first breakthrough late 1943; Feinstein first break of PURPLE 1940; Gardner professor of German linguistics (Univ. of Akron), Japanese in three months, taught himself Russian, reconstructing KGB codebook; 20 Dec 1946 message with list of Manhattan Project scientists, "proved the Manhattan Project had been infiltrated by Soviet spies".
- U.S. Army ICoE (L. S. Stewart), "Venona Officially Released to the Public (11 JUL 1995)", DVIDS 2026: https://www.dvidshub.net/news/575072 — collection since 1939; storage at Arlington Hall; 1 Feb 1943 Col. Carter Clarke; separate-peace fear; Grabeel 23, hired six weeks earlier; Weisband revealed effort 1948; no new messages after 1948; AFSA 1949, NSA 1952; ran to 1980; Lamphere 1986 book first public mention; 11 July 1995 ceremony at CIA, 49 translations; ~2,900 messages in all in six releases; copies to NARA, LoC, NCM; nearly 350 American CP members who spied.
- NSA Cryptologic Almanac, "VENONA: An Overview": https://media.defense.gov/2021/Jun/29/2002752012/-1/-1/0/VENONA_AN_OVERVIEW.PDF — Hallock Oct 1943; Feinstein Nov 1944; ~35,000 pages of duplicate key; ~3,000 messages partially read; secret-agent traffic most valuable; Bentley confirmed; "could only point investigations in the right direction"; ended 1 Oct 1980.
- R. L. Benson, "The VENONA Story" (NSA CCH): https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080920021555/http://www.nsa.gov/publications/publi00039.cfm — Gardner broke into a KGB message sent two years earlier on 20 Dec 1946; Clarke informed FBI late Aug/early Sept 1947; MLAD = Theodore Hall; CHARLES/REST = Fuchs (summer 1949); LIBERAL/ANTENNA; Weisband.
- Benson & Warner, eds., VENONA: Soviet Espionage and the American Response 1939–1957 (NSA/CIA 1996), intro and chronology: https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Venona-Front-Matter-Introduction-chronology-abbreviations.pdf — YOUNGSTER [MLAD] = Hall; "Special Agents questioned Hall in 1951, but he was never prosecuted"; "a case could not have been made without revealing AFSA's program"; Fuchs tied to REST/CHARLES 1949, author of a paper quoted in one message; 24 Jan 1950 Fuchs confesses; Gold arrested 22 May (1950); Lamphere–Gardner full-time liaison 19 Oct 1948; Moscow advising agents to flee via Mexico (various accounts); Weisband denied, never prosecuted.
- NSA, "A Highly Controversial Case of Espionage: A Summary" (19 June 2018): https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/history-today-articles/06%202018/19JUN2018%20A%20Highly%20Controversial%20Case%20of%20Espionage%20-%20A%20Summary.pdf — LIBERAL most active spy; Ethel knew; "do not suggest she committed any crimes worthy of capital punishment"; decrypts to stimulate investigations, "but never were to be used in court or otherwise made public"; Greenglass decade, released 1960; Gold released after 15 years.
- FBI, "Atom Spy Case/Rosenbergs": https://www.fbi.gov/history/famous-cases/atom-spy-caserosenbergs — Fuchs British team at Los Alamos; arrested 2 Feb 1950; 14 years, maximum under Official Secrets Act; Gold Philadelphia chemist, arrested May 1950; Greenglass Army enlisted man at Los Alamos 1944–45, Soviet agent; verdict 29 March 1951; executed 19 June 1953 at Sing Sing.
- D. P. Steury, "How the CIA Missed Stalin's Bomb," Studies in Intelligence 49:1 (2005): https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/CIA-Missed-Stalins-Bomb.pdf — by 1 July 1948 earliest possible mid-1950, most probable mid-1953; report disseminated 24 Aug 1949 still gave that projection; Joe-1 on 29 Aug 1949.
- U.S. Air Force: https://www.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/121177/aftac-continues-excellence-as-part-of-air-force-nuclear-enterprise ("In 1949, an Air Force B-29 Superfortress flying between Alaska and Japan detected debris from the first Russian atomic test"); AFTAC fact sheet (particulate sampler aboard an Air Weather Service modified B-29): https://www.patrick.spaceforce.mil/Portals/14/AFTAC%20Fact%20Sheet%20as%20of%20September%202018.pdf
- Truman announcement 23 Sept 1949: FRUS 1949 vol. I, editorial note (cited by the fact-checker): https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1949v01/d196 ; FBI page also refers to the September 23 statement.
