## 1. Kennedy's November 30, 1961 memorandum (start of the program)

**1.1 CLAIM:** Kennedy approved the program in writing on Nov 30, 1961. The goal was to help overthrow the "communist regime", with Lansdale as Chief of Operations.
- VERBATIM: "We will use our available assets to go ahead with the discussed project in order to help Cuba overthrow the communist regime."
- VERBATIM: "This program will be conducted under the general guidance of General Lansdale, acting as Chief of Operations. It will be conducted by him through the appropriate regular organizations and Departments of the government."
- VERBATIM: "The program will be reviewed in two weeks in order to determine whether General Lansdale will continue as Chief of Operations."
- VERBATIM (secrecy): "Knowledge of the existence of this operation should be restricted to the recipients of this memorandum, members of the 5412 group and the representatives appointed by the Secretaries and the Director."
- DOCUMENT: Memorandum From President Kennedy, Washington, November 30, 1961. Addressed to the Secretaries of State and Defense, the DCI, the Attorney General, Gen. Taylor, Gen. Lansdale, and Richard Goodwin. Top Secret; Eyes Only. FRUS 1961–63 vol. X, Doc. 278.
- URL OPENED: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1961-63v10/d278 (WebFetch, summary) plus the full text from the v10 TEI XML.
- NOTE: **The memo does not use the word "Mongoose."** It calls the effort "the Cuba Operation". The FRUS source note says it was "apparently drafted by McGeorge Bundy". It also says an earlier Nov 22 version had given Lansdale's responsibilities "to Attorney General Kennedy ... with Lansdale in a subordinate role as the Attorney Generalʼs Chief of Operations."

**1.2 CLAIM:** The codename and the founding White House meeting of Nov 3, 1961.
- VERBATIM (FRUS editors): "At a meeting in the White House on November 3, 1961, President Kennedy authorized the development of a new program designed to undermine the Castro government in Cuba. The program was codenamed Operation Mongoose."
- VERBATIM (Robert Kennedy's handwritten notes, quoted in the same note): "McNamara, Dick Bissell, Alexis Johnson, Paul Nitze, Lansdale (the Ugly American). McN said he would make latter available for me—I assigned him to make survey of situation in Cuba—the problem and our assets. My idea is to stir things up on island with espionage, sabotage, general disorder, run & operated by Cubans themselves with every group but Batistaites & Communists. Do not know if we will be successful in overthrowing Castro but we have nothing to lose in my estimate."
- VERBATIM (FRUS editors, on the command structure): "President Kennedy appointed Taylor as chairman of the group, but Robert Kennedy was the principal motive force within the group and the informal link between the group and the President."
- DOCUMENT: Editorial Note, FRUS 1961–63 vol. X, Doc. 270. The RFK notes are cited as Kennedy Library, RFK Attorney General Papers, Handwritten Notes, 11/7/61.
- URL OPENED: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1961-63v10/d270 (WebFetch confirmed both the "codenamed" sentence and the "stir things up" sentence word for word).

## 2. RFK, January 19, 1962: "no time, money, effort, or manpower is to be spared"

**2.1 CLAIM:** At a meeting he chaired on Jan 19, 1962, RFK called Cuba the government's top priority.
- VERBATIM (Helms memo version): "Accordingly, a solution to the Cuban problem today carries “The top priority in the United States Government—all else is secondary—no time, money, effort, or manpower is to be spared. There can be no misunderstanding on the involvement of the agencies concerned nor on their responsibility to carry out this job. The agency heads understand that you are to have full backing on what you need.”"
- VERBATIM: "Yesterday (18 January 1962), the President indicated to the Attorney General that “the final chapter on Cuba has not been written”—itʼs got to be done and will be done."
- VERBATIM: "“It is not only General Lansdaleʼs job to put the tasks, but yours to carry out with every resource at your command.”"
- DOCUMENT: Memorandum from Richard Helms (Chief of Operations, DDP) to DCI McCone, "Meeting with the Attorney General of the United States Concerning Cuba", Washington, January 19, 1962. Secret; Eyes Only. FRUS vol. X, Doc. 292. Attendees listed include Lansdale, Patchell, Gen. Craig, and George McManus (CIA). The memo notes: "(The Department of State was not represented although invited.)"
- URL: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1961-63v10/d292 (text from the v10 TEI XML).

**2.2 CLAIM:** The Church Committee quoted the McManus notes of the same meeting and recorded a split over what the words meant.
- VERBATIM (McManus notes as quoted): "Conclusion Overthrow of Castro is Possible." / "* * * a solution to the Cuban problem today carried top priority in U.S. Gov[ernmen]t. No time, money, effort—or manpower is to be spared."
- VERBATIM (report): "Helms stated that those words reflected the “kind of atmosphere” in which he had perceived that assassination was implicitly authorized."
- VERBATIM (report): "McManus agreed that Robert Kennedy “was very vehement in his speech” and “really wanted action,” but McManus disagreed with Helms' perception, stating that “it never occurred to me” that Kennedy's exhortation included permission to assassinate Castro"

## 3. Lansdale's "The Cuba Project" — 32 tasks (Jan 18) and the October 1962 timetable (Feb 20)

**3.1 CLAIM:** On Jan 18, 1962, Lansdale issued the program review "THE CUBA PROJECT" with 32 numbered tasks (Task 1 through Task 32).
- VERBATIM: "The U.S. objective is to help the Cubans overthrow the Communist regime from within Cuba and institute a new government with which the United States can live in peace."
- VERBATIM: "Basically, the operation is to bring about the revolt of the Cuban people."
- VERBATIM (endgame): "The United States, if possible in concert with other Western Hemisphere nations, will then give open support to the Cuban peoplesʼ revolt. Such support will include military force, as necessary."
- VERBATIM (sugar): "CIA was unable to undertake action to sabotage the sugar harvest, which commences about 15 January, and upon which Cubaʼs one-crop sugar economy depends. (Sabotage of transport, mills, sugar sacking and cane fields was explored.)"
- VERBATIM (TV): "Equipment to enable TV intrusion of Havana TV broadcasts has been reactivated on a small vessel under CIA control."
- VERBATIM (final task): "Task 32: Defense will submit plan for “special operations” use of" [continues in source]
- DOCUMENT: Program Review by the Chief of Operations, Operation Mongoose (Lansdale), Washington, January 18, 1962. Top Secret; Sensitive. 14 copies. FRUS vol. X, Doc. 291.
- URL: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1961-63v10/d291
- Church report on the same documents: "On January 18, 1962, Lansdale assigned 32 planning tasks to the agencies participating in MONGOOSE." A "Task 33" was added on Jan 19: "a plan to “incapacitate” Cuban sugar workers during the harvest by the use of chemical warfare means". It was later "cancelled without ever being submitted to the SGA for debate." The report also says: "The SGA approved Lansdale's 33 tasks for planning purposes on January 30, 1962." (S. Rep. 94-465, pp. 142–143; p.143 scan verified: https://archive.org/download/allegedassassina00unit/page/n164_w2000.jpg)
- Lansdale to his working group (quoted in the report, p.142): "it is our job to put the American genius to work on this project, quickly and effectively. This demands a change from the business as usual and a hard facing of the fact that we are in a combat situation—where we have been given full command." (Lansdale memorandum, 1/20/62)

**3.2 CLAIM:** On Feb 20, 1962, Lansdale set a six-phase timetable ending in "open revolt" in October 1962.
- VERBATIM: "It aims for a revolt which can take place in Cuba by October 1962. It is a series of target actions and dates, not a rigid time-table."
- VERBATIM (all six phases):
  "Phase I, Action, March 1962. Start moving in.
  Phase II, Build-up, April-July 1962. Activating the necessary operations inside Cuba for revolution and concurrently applying the vital political, economic, and military-type support from outside Cuba.
  Phase III, Readiness, 1 August 1962, check for final policy decision.
  Phase IV, Resistance, August-September 1962, move into guerrilla operations.
  Phase V, Revolt, first two weeks of October 1962. Open revolt and overthrow of the Communist regime.
  Phase VI, Final, during month of October 1962. Establishment of new government."
- VERBATIM: "Premise of Action. Americans once ran a successful revolution. It was run from within, and succeeded because there was timely and strong political, economic, and military help by nations outside who supported our cause."
- VERBATIM: "Time is running against us. The Cuban people feel helpless and are losing hope fast."
- VERBATIM: "On 18 January, the Chief of Operations assigned thirty-two tasks to Departments and Agencies of the U.S. government"
- VERBATIM (the key question): "will the U.S. respond promptly with military force to aid the Cuban revolt?"
- DOCUMENT: Program Review by the Chief of Operations, Operation Mongoose (Lansdale), "THE CUBA PROJECT", Washington, February 20, 1962. Top Secret; Sensitive. 12 copies, including one to the President through Bundy. FRUS vol. X, **Doc. 304**. The 39-page action plan is in the FRUS microfiche Supplement.
- URL: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1961-63v10/d304
- (Doc. 309 is a later Lansdale "Program Review" dated March 2, 1962. Do not confuse it with the Feb 20 review.)
- Church report (p.143): "On February 20, Lansdale detailed a six-phase schedule for MONGOOSE, designed to culminate in October, 1962, with an “open revolt and overthrow of the Communist regime.”" It also says the "Resistance" phase listed "attacks on the cadre of the regime, including key leaders", with the plan stating: "This should be a “Special Target” operation * * * Gangster elements might provide the best recruitment potential for actions against police—G2 [intelligence] officials." (p.143, scan n164 verified)
- Church report (p.144): "Lansdale's 33 plans were never approved for implementation by the SGA. As discussed below, the SGA tabled Lansdale's six phase plan altogether in February 1962, and directed him to plan for and conduct an intelligence collection plan only. (SGA Minutes, 3/5/62)"

## 4. Guidelines of March 14, 1962

**4.1 CLAIM:** The official guidelines assumed that only U.S. military intervention could finish the job.
- VERBATIM: "In undertaking to cause the overthrow of the target government, the U.S. will make maximum use of indigenous resources, internal and external, but recognizes that final success will require decisive U.S. military intervention."
- VERBATIM: "Such indigenous resources as are developed will be used to prepare for and justify this intervention, and thereafter to facilitate and support it."
- VERBATIM: "The immediate priority objective of U.S. efforts during the coming months will be the acquisition of hard intelligence on the target area. Concurrently, all other political, economic and covert actions will be undertaken short of those reasonably calculated to inspire a revolt within the target area, or other development which would require U.S. armed intervention."
- VERBATIM: "...will be taken in such a way as to permit disengagement with minimum losses in assets and U.S. prestige."
- VERBATIM (Kennedy's reaction, from U. Alexis Johnson's March 16 note quoted in the source note): "“The President also expressed skepticism that in so far as can now be foreseen circumstances will arise that would justify and make desirable the use of American forces for overt military action. It was clearly understood no decision was expressed or implied approving the use of such forces although contingency planning would proceed.”"
- DOCUMENT: Guidelines for Operation Mongoose, Washington, March 14, 1962. Drafted by Gen. Taylor on March 5. FRUS vol. X, Doc. 314.
- URL: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1961-63v10/d314

## 5. August 1962: "Stepped Up Course B" and NSAM 181 ("Mongoose Plan B plus")

**5.1 CLAIM:** Lansdale's Aug 8, 1962 paper proposed lifting the "short of revolt" restriction. The CIA's own operators believed success would need U.S. troops.
- VERBATIM: "SUBJECT Stepped Up Course B"
- VERBATIM (Course B definition): "“b. Exert all possible diplomatic, economic, psychological, and other pressures to overthrow the Castro-Communist regime, without overt employment of U.S. military.”"
- VERBATIM: "The major difference from Phase I of Operation Mongoose would be in removing the restriction, in the 14 March policy guidelines, which kept our actions “short of those reasonably calculated to inspire a revolt within the target area.”"
- VERBATIM: "The CIA operational people, who would implement a stepped up Course B as Phase II of Operation Mongoose, do not believe this course of action by itself would bring the overthrow of the regime in Cuba; they believe that the use of U.S. military force in the final stage must be anticipated, for success."
- VERBATIM (approvals needed): "authority would be required for overflights (resupply, leaflet drops, infiltration, exfiltration), use of U.S. submarines for infiltration and exfiltration, major sabotage operations, stepped up psychological actions (including propaganda balloons and establishing a medium-wave Radio Free Cuba), training Cubans on U.S. bases, some operational use of Guantanamo..."
- VERBATIM (Harvey attachment "COVERT ACTIVITIES", Aug 7): "Conduct maximum possible sabotage of major Cuban industries and public utilities with priority attention being given to transportation, communications, power plants, and utilities. No sabotage would be undertaken against food supplies, medical facilities, or directly against the population of Cuba as such."
- VERBATIM (Harvey assumptions): "Soviet troops will not be present in Cuba in force."
- DOCUMENT: Memorandum From the Chief of Operations, Operation Mongoose (Lansdale) to the Special Group (Augmented), Washington, August 8, 1962. 16 copies. FRUS vol. X, Doc. 367.
- URL: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1961-63v10/d367

**5.2 CLAIM:** On Aug 23, 1962, Kennedy ordered "Mongoose Plan B plus" sped up. The same memo ordered studies on missiles in Cuba.
- VERBATIM: "The line of activity projected for Operation Mongoose Plan B plus should be developed with all possible speed. (Action: General Taylor)"
- VERBATIM: "An analysis should be prepared of the probable military, political and psychological impact of the establishment in Cuba of either surface-to-air missiles or surface-to-surface missiles which could reach the U.S."
- VERBATIM: "What action can be taken to get Jupiter missiles out of Turkey?"
- DOCUMENT: National Security Action Memorandum No. 181, Washington, August 23, 1962. FRUS vol. X, Doc. 386.
- URL: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1961-63v10/d386

## 6. Task Force W and JM/WAVE: size

**6.1 CLAIM:** About 400 people worked on Mongoose at CIA headquarters and the Miami station.
- VERBATIM: "In late 1961 or early 1962, William Harvey was put in charge of the CIA's Task Force W, the CIA unit for MONGOOSE Operations. Task Force W operated under guidance from the Special Group (Augmented) and employed a total of approximately 400 people at CIA headquarters and its Miami Station."
- VERBATIM: "Although Helms attended only 7 of the 40 MONGOOSE meetings, he was significantly involved"
- DOCUMENT: S. Rep. 94-465, p. 140.
- URL OPENED: archive.org djvu.txt. Scan: https://archive.org/download/allegedassassina00unit/page/n161_w2000.jpg (HTTP 200, image/jpeg; header "140" verified)
- Repeated by FRUS editors (vol. X, Doc. 270): "Within the CIA, the Mongoose operation was run by Task Force W, under the direction of William Harvey ... The CIA developed an operational force of approximately 400 people at CIA headquarters and at its Miami Station". Cited to the Church report p.140.
- Church report p.153: "Early in 1962, Harvey was appointed chief of Task Force W, CIA's action arm for MONGOOSE activities. In the latter part of April 1962, Harvey went to Miami where the CIA had its JM/WAVE station."
- NSArchive 2019 (editors' text, secondary): "The agency's station in Miami, JM/WAVE, expanded rapidly." and "The revised plan anticipated increasing CIA personnel involved to over 600". The second quote refers to a revised CIA plan of August 1962, not a JM/WAVE headcount. The page also mentions "a $13.8 million budget for Fiscal Year 1962" in a 1961 CIA proposal, which was later cut.
- **NOT FOUND / NOT VERIFIED:** I found no official figure for JM/WAVE boats, budget, or station headcount (e.g., the often-repeated "300–400 case officers", "largest station", or "$50 million a year"). ## 7. "Elimination by Illumination"

**7.1 CLAIM:** Thomas Parrott, the Special Group's secretary, testified that Lansdale proposed faking the Second Coming with a submarine firing star shells off Cuba.
- VERBATIM (footnote 2, verified against the page scan): "Parrott sarcastically characterized Lansdale's plans as follows: “I'll give you one example of Lansdale's perspicacity. He had a wonderful plan for getting rid of Castro. This plan consisted of spreading the word that the Second Coming of Christ was imminent and that Christ was against Castro (who) was anti-Christ. And you would spread this word around Cuba, and then on whatever date it was, that there would be a manifestation of this thing. And at that time—this is absolutely true—and at that time just over the horizon there would be an American submarine which would surface off of Cuba and send up some starshells. And this would be the manifestation of the Second Coming and Castro would be overthrown * * * Well, some wag called this operation—and somebody dubbed this—Elimination by Illumination.” (Parrott, 7/10/75, pp. 49, 50)"
- DOCUMENT: S. Rep. 94-465, p. 142, footnote 2. Parrott testimony, July 10, 1975.
- SCAN (verified visually): https://archive.org/download/allegedassassina00unit/page/n163_w2000.jpg
- CAVEAT for script: this is one witness's sarcastic recollection 13 years later. I found no Lansdale planning document with this name, so attribute it to Parrott's testimony. The same footnote also has McNamara: "we were hysterical about Castro at the time of the Bay of Pigs and thereafter." and "I don't believe we contemplated assassination."

## 8. Assassination plots overlapping Mongoose (Church Committee)

**8.1 CLAIM:** The Committee's overall finding was that it could not establish presidential authorization.
- VERBATIM: "The Committee finds that the system of executive command and control was so ambiguous that it is difficult to be certain at what levels assassination activity was known and authorized. This situation creates the disturbing prospect that Government officials might have undertaken the assassination plots without it having been uncontrovertibly clear that there was explicit authorization from the Presidents. It is also possible that there might have been a successful “plausible denial” in which Presidential authorization was issued but is now obscured. Whether or not the respective Presidents knew of or authorized the plots, as chief executive officer of the United States, each must bear the ultimate responsibility for the activities of his subordinates."
- VERBATIM: "The first relates to the Committee's inability to make a finding that the assassination plots were authorized by the Presidents or other persons above the governmental agency or agencies involved."
- VERBATIM (context): "the plots against Fidel Castro personally cannot be understood without considering the fully authorized, comprehensive assaults upon his regime, such as the Bay of Pigs invasion in 1961 and Operation MONGOOSE in 1962."
- VERBATIM: "Words of urgency which may have meant killing to the former, may have meant nothing of the sort to the latter."
- DOCUMENT: S. Rep. 94-465, Summary, pp. 6–7. The page break falls inside "uncon-/trovertibly".
- SCANS: p.6 https://archive.org/download/allegedassassina00unit/page/n27_w2000.jpg ; p.7 https://archive.org/download/allegedassassina00unit/page/n28_w2000.jpg (both HTTP 200; headers "6" and "7" verified)

**8.2 CLAIM:** The Committee found at least eight CIA plots against Castro between 1960 and 1965.
- VERBATIM: "We have found concrete evidence of at least eight plots involving the CIA to assassinate Fidel Castro from 1960 to 1965. Although some of the assassination plots did not advance beyond the stage of planning and preparation, one plot, involving the use of underworld figures, reportedly twice progressed to the point of sending poison pills to Cuba and dispatching teams to commit the deed."
- VERBATIM: "The proposed assassination devices ran the gamut from high-powered rifles to poison pills, poison pens, deadly bacterial powders, and other devices which strain the imagination."
- DOCUMENT: S. Rep. 94-465, p. 70 (section "The Assassination Plots"). Scan: https://archive.org/download/allegedassassina00unit/page/n91_w2000.jpg (HTTP 200; header "70" viewed)

**8.3 CLAIM:** Harvey (Task Force W chief) ran the Rosselli channel during Mongoose. He passed weapons through JM/WAVE and ended the operation in Feb 1963.
- VERBATIM: "With the help of the CIA's Miami station which ran covert operations against Cuba (JM/WAVE), Harvey procured explosives, detonators, rifles, handguns, radios, and boat radar costing about $5,000. (I.G. Report, p. 49) Harvey and the chief of the JM/WAVE station rented a U-Haul truck under an assumed name and delivered the equipment to a parking lot."
- VERBATIM: "Rosselli testified that he told Harvey that the Cubans intended to use the pills to assassinate Che Guevara as well as Fidel and Raul Castro. According to Rosselli's testimony, Harvey approved of the targets, stating “everything is all right, what they want to do.”"
- VERBATIM: "Harvey terminated the operation in mid-February 1963." / (p.85) "However, he never informed them that the offer of $150,000 for Castro's assassination had been withdrawn."
- DOCUMENT: S. Rep. 94-465, pp. 84–85. Scan p.84: https://archive.org/download/allegedassassina00unit/page/n105_w2000.jpg (verified header "84")
- Related FRUS (vol. X, Doc. 337, editors' note quoting the 1967 CIA IG report): "CIA twice (first in early 1961 and again in early 1962) supplied lethal pills to U.S. gambling syndicate members working on behalf of CIA in a plot to assassinate Fidel Castro." https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1961-63v10/d337
- Same note, JFK to Tad Szulc on Nov 9, 1961: "“What would you think if I ordered Castro to be assassinated?”" ... "Kennedy responded, “Iʼm glad you feel the same way.”" (Szulc's own notes; FRUS says these are in the JFK Collection)

**8.4 CLAIM:** On Aug 10, 1962, assassination was raised at the Special Group (Augmented). Harvey cut "liquidation of leaders" out of Lansdale's follow-up memo.
- VERBATIM (report): "The question of liquidating Cuban leaders was raised at a meeting of the SGA on August 10, 1962. On August 13, 1962, Lansdale directed Harvey to include in a proposed plan for Phase II of MONGOOSE, an option for the “liquidation of leaders.”"
- VERBATIM (Harvey to Helms, 8/14/62, as quoted): "The question of assassination, particularly of Fidel Castro, was brought up by Secretary McNamara at the meeting of the Special Group (Augmented) in Secretary Rusk's office on 10 August."
- VERBATIM: Harvey pointed out "the inadmissability [sic] and stupidity of putting this type of comment in writing in such a document." He also said CIA "would write no document pertaining to this and would participate in no open meeting discussing it."
- VERBATIM: "The minutes of the August 10 meeting contain no reference to assassination."
- VERBATIM (IG finding, quoted): "The subject (of a Castro assassination) was raised at a meeting at State on 10 August 1962, but is unrelated to any actual attempts at assassination."
- DOCUMENT: S. Rep. 94-465, pp. 161–162. Scans: p.161 https://archive.org/download/allegedassassina00unit/page/n182_w2000.jpg ; p.162 https://archive.org/download/allegedassassina00unit/page/n183_w2000.jpg (HTTP 200)

**8.5 CLAIM:** RFK was briefed on the CIA–Mafia plot in May 1962 and told it had ended. It had not.
- VERBATIM (IG report as quoted): "The Attorney General was not told that the gambling syndicate operation had already been reactivated, nor, as far as we know, was he ever told that CIA had a continuing involvement with U.S. gangster elements. (I.G. Report, p. 65)"

## 9. The missile crisis overlap, and the end

**9.1 CLAIM:** On Oct 4 and Oct 16, 1962, RFK pressed for more sabotage. On the morning of Oct 16, the day the missiles were briefed, he relayed the President's "general dissatisfaction."
- VERBATIM (Oct 4, McCone memo): "The Attorney General reported on discussions with the President on Cuba; dissatisfied with lack of action in the sabotage field, went on to stress that nothing was moving forward". It continues: "General Lansdale was instructed to give consideration to new and more dynamic approaches, the specific items of sabotage should be brought forward immediately and new ones conceived, that a plan for mining harbors should be developed and presented, and the possibility of capturing Castro forces for interrogation should be studied." - VERBATIM (Oct 16, Helms memo): "The Attorney General opened the meeting by expressing the “general dissatisfaction of the President” with Operation Mongoose. "he will hold a meeting every morning at 0930 with the Mongoose operational representatives". **9.3 CLAIM:** Mongoose ended after the crisis. Bundy wrote on Jan 4, 1963 that it was "at a dead end."
- VERBATIM (Nov 29, 1962 ExComm, McCone): "DCI stated he felt future activity should be restricted to intelligence gathering" ... "DCI also stated that the form of Mongoose organization should be modified and this was agreed". FRUS vol. XI, Doc. 217. https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1961-63v11/d217
- VERBATIM (Bundy to JFK, Jan 4, 1963): "there is well nigh universal agreement that Mongoose is at a dead end." / "we think the Mongoose office should be disbanded and responsibility for covert operations should be a part of the work of the Coordinator" / "Such a change would liberate General Lansdale for many other tasks in which his services are uniquely valuable." FRUS vol. XI, Doc. 261. https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1961-63v11/d261
- VERBATIM (Church, Baker views p.337): "While the MONGOOSE program ended around the time of the Cuban missile crisis in October 1962, sabotage continued."
- NOTE: I found no single document with a formal termination date. FRUS supports this sequence: stand-down Oct 26–30, 1962 → "modified" Nov 29, 1962 → recommendation to disband Jan 4, 1963.

**9.4 CLAIM:** Khrushchev cited the threat of U.S. attack on Cuba as the reason for Soviet aid.
- VERBATIM (Embassy translation): "You once said that the United States was not preparing an invasion. But you also declared that you sympathized with the Cuban counter-revolutionary emigrants, that you support them and would help them to realize their plans against the present Government of Cuba. It is also not a secret to anyone that the threat of armed attack, aggression, has constantly hung, and continues to hand [sic] over Cuba. It was only this which impelled us to respond to the request of the Cuban Government to furnish it aid for the strengthening of the defensive capacity of this country."
- VERBATIM: "You would declare that the United States will not invade Cuba with its forces and will not support any sort of forces which might intend to carry out an invasion of Cuba. Then the necessity for the presence of our military specialists in Cuba would disappear."
- DOCUMENT: Khrushchev to Kennedy, Moscow, October 26, 1962, 7 p.m. (Embassy Moscow translation telegram). FRUS vol. XI, Doc. 84. https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1961-63v11/d84
- CAVEAT: Khrushchev does not name Mongoose and could not have known its name. The script may say he cited the "threat of armed attack". It must not say he cited Mongoose.
