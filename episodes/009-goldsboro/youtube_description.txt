Just after midnight on January 24, 1961, a B-52 on airborne alert broke apart over Wayne County, North Carolina, carrying two Mark 39 thermonuclear bombs. One came down under its parachute and was found hanging in a tree; the other broke apart in waterlogged farmland, and part of it was never recovered. We read the files: the Pentagon's 1981 accident summary, Sandia's February 1961 analysis of what the crash set off inside each bomb, Defense Secretary McNamara's "two wires" remark, and the 1969 Sandia memo "Goldsboro Revisited," which said one low-voltage switch stood between the United States and a major catastrophe.

Facts come from documents and official sources. Claims are attributed. Theories are labelled as theories.
