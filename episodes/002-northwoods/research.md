## A. The memo itself (primary)
- **Full 15-page JCS memo "Justification for US Military Intervention in Cuba (TS)", 13 March 1962** — National Security Archive PDF (created May 2001), mirrored on archive.org: https://archive.org/download/northwoods_202610/Northwoods.pdf (OCR text: https://archive.org/download/northwoods_202610/Northwoods_djvu.txt; local copy `memo_ocr.txt`). Pages read visually (scans) in this run.
  - p.1 cover memo: "THE JOINT CHIEFS OF STAFF, WASHINGTON 25, D.C." · "13 March 1962" · "MEMORANDUM FOR THE SECRETARY OF DEFENSE" · Subject: "Justification for US Military Intervention in Cuba (TS)". Para 1: responds to a request of the Chief of Operations, Cuba Project "for brief but precise description of pretexts which would provide justification for US military intervention in Cuba." Para 2: "recommend that the proposed memorandum be forwarded as a preliminary submission suitable for planning purposes… Individual projects can then be considered on a case-by-case basis." Para 3: "…recommended that this responsibility for both overt and covert military operations be assigned the Joint Chiefs of Staff." Signed "For the Joint Chiefs of Staff: L. L. LEMNITZER, Chairman, Joint Chiefs of Staff".
  - p.2 "JCS 1969/321 … 12 March 1962 … NOTE BY THE SECRETARIES to the JOINT CHIEFS OF STAFF on NORTHWOODS (S)" (code word).
  - p.4–6 report (9 March 1962) by DoD/JCS representative on the Caribbean Survey Group: "Cognizance has been taken of a suggested course of action proposed** by the US Navy relating to generated instances in the Guantanamo area." Footnote *: "Memorandum for General Craig from Chief of Operations, Cuba Project, subject: 'Operation MONGOOSE', dated 5 March 1962". Footnote **: "Memorandum for the Chairman, Joint Chiefs of Staff, from Chief of Naval Operations, subject: 'Instances to Provoke Military Actions in Cuba (TS)', dated 8 March 1962".
  - Discussion para 6: premise "will continue to hold good only as long as there can be reasonable certainty that US military intervention in Cuba would not directly involve the Soviet Union… all projects are suggested within the time frame of the next few months."
  - Recommendations 8 b–d: "This paper NOT be forwarded to commanders of unified or specified commands." / "…NOT be forwarded to US officers assigned to NATO activities." / "…NOT be forwarded to the Chairman, US Delegation, United Nations Military Staff Committee."
  - Appendix para 3: "…to place the United States in the apparent position of suffering defensible grievances from a rash and irresponsible government of Cuba…"
  - Annex "PRETEXTS TO JUSTIFY US MILITARY INTERVENTION IN CUBA", 9 numbered courses of action:
    - 2a Guantanamo: "(1) Start rumors (many)…", "(2) Land friendly Cubans in uniform 'over-the-fence' to stage attack on base.", "(4) Start riots near the base main gate (friendly Cubans).", "(5) Blow up ammunition inside the base; start fires.", "(6) Burn aircraft on air base (sabotage).", "(7) Lob mortar shells from outside of base into base.", "(11) Sink ship near harbor entrance. Conduct funerals for mock-victims (may be lieu of (10))." 2c: "Commence large scale United States military operations."
    - 3: "A 'Remember the Maine' incident could be arranged in several forms: a. We could blow up a US ship in Guantanamo Bay and blame Cuba. b. We could blow up a drone (unmanned) vessel anywhere in the Cuban waters… in the vicinity of Havana or Santiago… 'evacuate' remaining members of the non-existent crew. Casualty lists in US newspapers would cause a helpful wave of national indignation."
    - 4: "We could develop a Communist Cuban terror campaign in the Miami area, in other Florida cities and even in Washington… We could sink a boatload of Cubans enroute to Florida (real or simulated). We could foster attempts on lives of Cuban refugees in the United States even to the extent of wounding in instances to be widely publicized. Exploding a few plastic bombs in carefully chosen spots, the arrest of Cuban agents and the release of prepared documents substantiating Cuban involvement…"
    - 6: "Use of MIG type aircraft by US pilots could provide additional provocation. Harassment of civil air, attacks on surface shipping and destruction of US military drone aircraft by MIG type planes… An F-86 properly painted would convince air passengers that they saw a Cuban MIG…"
    - 8: "…a Cuban aircraft has attacked and shot down a chartered civil airliner enroute from the United States to Jamaica, Guatemala, Panama or Venezuela. The destination would be chosen only to cause the flight plan route to cross Cuba. The passengers could be a group of college students off on a holiday…" a: "An aircraft at Eglin AFB would be painted and numbered as an exact duplicate for a civil registered aircraft belonging to a CIA proprietary organization in the Miami area… loaded with the selected passengers, all boarded under carefully prepared aliases. The actual registered aircraft would be converted to a drone." b: rendezvous south of Florida; passenger aircraft "will descend to minimum altitude and go directly into an auxiliary field at Eglin AFB"; drone "transmitting on the international distress frequency a 'MAY DAY' message stating he is under attack by Cuban MIG aircraft… destruction of the aircraft which will be triggered by radio signal. This will allow ICAO radio stations in the Western Hemisphere to tell the US what has happened to the aircraft instead of the US trying to 'sell' the incident."
    - 9: "Approximately 4 or 5 F-101 aircraft will be dispatched in trail from Homestead AFB, Florida, to the vicinity of Cuba… a pre-briefed pilot… would broadcast that he had been jumped by MIGs and was going down… fly directly west at extremely low altitude and land at a secure base, an Eglin auxiliary… given a new tail number… a submarine or small surface craft would disburse F-101 parts, parachute, etc., at approximately 15 to 20 miles off the Cuban coast… Search ships and aircraft could be dispatched and parts of aircraft found."
  - Enclosure B para 2 quoting JCS on 7 March 1962 (JCS 1969/313): "determination that a credible internal revolt is impossible of attainment during the next 9-10 months will require a decision by the United States to develop a Cuban 'provocation' as justification for positive US military action."
- NSArchive item page (2022 CMC-60 collection): https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/CMC-60/joint-chiefs-pretexts-to-invade-cuba-1962 — bears the code word "Northwoods"; became public through the JFK Assassination Records Review Board in the 1990s; NSArchive published online in 2001.

## B. Context: Bay of Pigs, Mongoose, Lansdale
- JCS official biography of Gen. Lyman L. Lemnitzer: https://www.jcs.mil/About/The-Joint-Staff/Chairman/General-Lyman-Louis-Lemnitzer — Chairman Oct 1, 1960 – Sep 30, 1962; CINCEUR from Nov 1, 1962; SACEUR from Jan 1, 1963; CIA-trained brigade of exiles landed at Bay of Pigs and was quickly defeated; Kennedy felt the Chiefs had not reviewed the plan thoroughly or voiced reservations clearly; Kennedy did not reappoint him, nominated him as SACEUR.
