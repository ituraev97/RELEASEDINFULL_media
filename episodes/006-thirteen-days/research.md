## A. CIA History Staff, *CIA Documents on the Cuban Missile Crisis, 1962* (ed. Mary S. McAuliffe, 1992) — primary documents
- Copy read: https://archive.org/details/DTIC_ADA498025 (DTIC ADA498025, PDF 355 pp. + OCR). Local: /home/claude/src/docs/cia92.pdf, cia92.txt; page renders in /home/claude/src/docs/hi/.
- Foreword: McCone "was away from Washington on his honeymoon in France from the evening of 23 August through 23 September 1962"; publication 1992 ("30 years that have elapsed since those 13 days in October"); documents "subject to declassification review".
- Doc 1, "Maps of Cuban overflights, August–October 1962" (pdf p. 27): map "14 OCTOBER FLIGH[T]" with lines labelled "CIA PLANNED ROUTE" and "ACTUAL ROUTE FLOWN BY SAC", "TARGET AREA", "PROB. OPERATIONAL SAM SITE". pdf p. 28: map "CUBA U-2 OVERFLIGHT BY SAC 15-22 OCTOBER 62".
- Doc 4, John A. McCone, memorandum "Soviet MRBMs in Cuba," 31 Oct 1962 (pdf p. 36, printed p. 14): "8. On September 10th McCone wired Carter [redacted] as follows: 'Difficult for me to rationalize extensive costly defenses being established in Cuba … Appears to me quite possible measures now being taken are for purpose of insuring secrecy of some offensive capability such as MRBMs to be installed by Soviets after present phase completed and country secured from overflights. Suggest BNE study motives behind these defensive measures …'" Para 9: BNE "continued to feel that the installation of SA 2s is most reasonably explained by other than a desire to hide MRBM build-up."
- Memorandum to Acting DCI on U-2 photography of 29 August (OCR lines ~2675–2700): "U-2 photography of 29 August confirms extensive Soviet military deliveries to Cuba … The photography shows eight SAM sites being set up … the speed of the work indicate the program is proceeding on a crash basis."
- Doc 12, Kirkpatrick memo (OCR ~2955–3020): McCone 16 Sept cable "we must carefully study the prospect of MRBM's in Cuba"; DD/I reply 18 Sept "an introduction of MRBM's was unlikely because of the risk of U. S. intervention."
- Doc 33, SNIE 85-3-62, "The Military Buildup in Cuba," 19 September 1962 (pdf p. 100 cover, p. 102 conclusions): para D: "The USSR could derive considerable military advantage from the establishment of Soviet medium and intermediate range ballistic missiles in Cuba … Either development, however, would be incompatible with Soviet practice to date and with Soviet policy as we presently estimate it." Para B mentions "the Soviet statement of 11 September".
- Inspector General-type review (OCR ~5536): "by about 1 October, the San Cristobal area had been pinpointed as a suspect MRBM site and photographic confirmation had been requested."
- Doc 46, CIA memorandum "Probable Soviet MRBM Sites in Cuba," 16 October 1962 (pdf pp. 143–147): "Photography of 14 October 1962 has disclosed two areas in the Sierra del Rosario mountains about 50 n.m. west southwest of Havana which appear to contain Soviet MRBMs in the early stages of deployment"; "six canvas-covered trailers of 80 feet in overall length which are of the general size and configuration of those used to transport the Soviet SS-3 (700 n.m. ballistic missile) and SS-4 (1100 n.m. ballistic missile)"; "No facility to store nuclear warheads can be identified at any of these three installations"; "Both the SS-3 and SS-4 are single stage vehicles which will carry a 3,000 lb. warhead to a maximum range of 700 n.m. and 1100 n.m. respectively"; "Both of these systems are road-mobile and can be deployed with no heavy construction work". Range map (pdf p. 147): rings "350 NM", "630 NM", "1020 NM" centered on San Diego de los Baños; the 1020 NM ring passes Washington.
- Doc 50, Arthur C. Lundahl (Director, NPIC), memorandum "Additional Information — Mission 3101," 16 October 1962 (pdf p. 156): "An examination of photography from Mission 3101 dated 14 October 1962 has revealed an MRBM Launch Site and two new military encampments …"; "a total of at least 14 canvas-covered missile trailers measuring approximately 67 feet in length and 9 feet in width. The overall length of the trailers including the tow bar is approximately 80 feet"; Area 1: "at least 8 canvas-covered missile trailers and 4 deployed probable missile erector/launchers (unrevetted) … The westernmost position has a missile tractor/trailer aligned with the erector"; Area 2 "5.8 NM north of Los Palacios … at least 6 canvas-covered missile trailers".

## B. Foreign Relations of the United States, 1961–1963, Vol. XI, Doc. 16 (Editorial Note)
- https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1961-63v11/d16 (read via web reader): Oct 14, 1962, Air Force Major Richard D. Heyser flew a U-2 over western Cuba; "6-minute flight" produced "928 photographs"; NPIC identified three MRBM sites near San Cristobal, eight large MRBM transporters, four erector launchers in tentative firing positions; Oct 15: two more U-2 missions (SAC pilots) found a fourth MRBM site near San Cristobal, two IRBM sites at Guanajay, 21 crates for IL-28 bombers at San Julian airfield; 8:30 p.m. Oct 15 Carter reported to Bundy; Bundy did not wake the President; reasons (March 4, 1963 memo, printed in *Danger and Survival*): materials not ready before morning; President tired, returned from Niagara Falls/New York at 1:40 a.m. Monday; "a quiet evening and a night of sleep were the best preparation".

"Yes or no—don't wait for the translation—yes or no?" ## D. State Department, Office of the Historian, Milestones: "The Cuban Missile Crisis, October 1962"
- https://history.state.gov/milestones/1961-1968/cuban-missile-crisis: Oct 14 U-2 photographed MRBM/IRBM sites under construction; images to White House next day; "all the Joint Chiefs" favored an air strike followed by invasion; Kennedy chose a middle course; Oct 22 ordered a naval "quarantine" — the term "legally distinguished this action from a blockade"; Joint Chiefs set DEFCON 3; Oct 26 Khrushchev's long, emotional message ("let us take measures to untie that knot"); Oct 27 second message demanded removal of Jupiters from Turkey; U-2 shot down over Cuba the same day; Kennedy responded to the first message (UN-supervised removal, guarantee against attack on Cuba); RFK met Dobrynin secretly: Jupiters to be removed soon anyway but not part of public resolution; Jupiters removed April 1963; Oct 28 Khrushchev announced dismantling; quarantine until IL-28 removal agreed; ended Nov 20, 1962; leaders struggled to understand each other's intentions → "Hotline".

## E. President Kennedy, address of Oct 22, 1962
- Catalog/audio: https://jfklibrary.org/learn/about-jfk/historic-speeches/address-during-the-cuban-missile-crisis (date 22 Oct 1962; ~18 min).
- Text read at https://www.emersonkent.com/speeches/cuban_missile_crisis.htm and cross-checked at https://worldjpn.net/documents/texts/docs/19621022.S1E.html (citing *Modern International Relations: Basic Documents*): "Several of them include medium-range ballistic missiles capable of carrying a nuclear warhead for a distance of more than 1,000 nautical miles"; "Additional sites not yet completed appear to be designed for intermediate range ballistic missiles" able to strike most major cities in the Western Hemisphere; "a strict quarantine on all offensive military equipment under shipment to Cuba is being initiated"; "It shall be the policy of this nation to regard any nuclear missile launched from Cuba against any nation in the Western Hemisphere as an attack by the Soviet Union on the United States, requiring a full retaliatory response upon the Soviet Union"; "The path we have chosen for the present is full of hazards, as all paths are"; "my own public warnings to the Soviets on September 4 and 13"; "repeated assurances of Soviet spokesmen … that the arms buildup in Cuba would retain its original defensive character, and that the Soviet Union had no need or desire to station strategic missiles on the territory of any other nation".

## F. White House meeting transcript, Oct 16, 1962, 6:30–7:55 p.m.
- https://microsites.jfklibrary.org/cmc/oct16/doc3.html ("Off the Record Meeting on Cuba"): Carter: "We have no evidence whatsoever of any nuclear warhead storage near the field launchers." Carter: "our people estimate that these could be fully operational within two weeks." Carter on identification: comparison "with the characteristics of our own missiles as to size and length and diameter."

## I. Soviet nuclear weapons in Cuba (later revelations)
- National Security Archive, EBB No. 449, "Last Nuclear Weapons Left Cuba in December 1962" (Dec 11, 2013): https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB449/ — "in total, 98 tactical nuclear warheads" (80 FKR-1 cruise missile, 12 Luna, 6 IL-28 bombs); "almost 43,000 troops and all the nuclear warheads were still in Cuba" [at the time of the Oct 28 settlement]; Gen. Anatoly Gribkov revealed their presence at the January 1992 Havana conference; "U.S. intelligence did not detect any of the nuclear warheads in Cuba during the crisis."
