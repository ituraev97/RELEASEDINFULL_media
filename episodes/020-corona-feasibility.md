# 020 CORONA - Feasibility + Fact Sheet (2026-11-25)

VERDICT: Script feasible (facts well sourced, all government). Footage is the weak point: only ~17 min of public-domain-ish moving footage was found, and the best item is NOT clearly PD. See section 2. Not verified: any NARA/DVIDS CORONA-specific film.

## 1. Facts (URL opened -> claim)

- https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/corona-declassified : "On February 24, 1995, Vice President Gore visited CIA Headquarters to announce Executive Order 12951". "CORONA collected over 800,000 images". "Known to the public as the U.S. Air Force's Discoverer program". Each image covered "approximately 10x120 miles." No resolution or mission count on this page.
- https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/executive-order-12951-release-imagery-acquired-space-based-national-intelligence : EO signed "February 22, 1995" (note: 22 signing vs 24 announcement; use carefully). Covers Corona, Argon, Lanyard imagery, to be transferred to the National Archives within 18 months; "shall be deemed declassified and shall be made available to the public." No image count in the order.
- https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Corona_Declassification-1996.pdf : scope "over 2 million linear feet with some 800,000 photographs"; at the 1995 ceremony four early images were released. It does not confirm the total actually released in 1995 (do not state a "released in 1995" number beyond 800,000 photographs in scope).
- https://www.cia.gov/legacy/museum/exhibit/corona-americas-first-imaging-satellite-program/ : "The first 13 missions failed to return any useable imagery." "on 18 August 1960, all systems on CORONA Mission XIV operated successfully!" Plan to "snag the capsule in midair over the Pacific Ocean." "The 145th and final CORONA launch took place on 25 May 1972" (last recovery 31 May 1972). Imagery "dispelled both the missile gap and the bomber gap." U-2: "CIA pilot Francis Gary Powers near Sverdlovsk on 1 May 1960" shot down; Eisenhower halted overflights. WARNING: page is internally inconsistent (video says 72 million sq mi; article says 750,000,000; video says imagery "1968 up until 1972"). Do not cite its area figures.
- https://www.nro.gov/About-NRO/history/history-corona/ : "Twelve failed, and a thirteenth was a test vehicle that carried no film." "The first fully successful mission was launched on 18 August 1960 and recovered the next day." About 3,000 feet of film. "almost twelve years and 145 missions". Resolution "about 40 feet with the KH-1 to 5-7 feet with the KH-4". Eisenhower approved 7 Feb 1958. "declassified in 1995 by an executive order from President Clinton." This resolves the "12 failures" claim: 12 failures + 1 no-camera flight (Discoverer XIII) before XIV.
- https://www.army.mil/article/173155/project_corona_americas_first_photo_reconnaissance_satellite (Army, secondary): "Flight XIV covered more than 1,650,000 square miles of Soviet territory" and "produced more images than all of the earlier U-2 missions combined." Discoverer XIII "completed 17 orbits", "not equipped with a camera", "the first man-made object recovered from an orbiting satellite." Initial camera "35-40 feet". Calls it "Corona XIV" and says mid-air recovery was 19 Aug 1960. NOTE the Army, NRO and CIA say launch 18 Aug / recovery 19 Aug. The user's "Aug 19 mid-air recovery" is correct for recovery.
- https://www.cia.gov/static/Corona-Between-the-Sun-and-the-Earth.pdf (CIA CSI monograph, first 100k of 471k chars read only): DISCOVERER cover story presented as vehicle tests then space-environment research, and "had simply worn out" after 37 launches/attempts. Mission XIV "provided more photographic coverage of the Soviet Union than all previous U-2 missions"; covered areas "the U-2 had never reached," at lower resolution than U-2. "of the first 30 missions from 1960 through 1962, only 12 were considered productive" (the CIA monograph's 12 is DIFFERENT from "12 failures": do not conflate). Eisenhower and Dulles inspected the photos six days after recovery; the flag from XIII's capsule was given to Eisenhower. KH-1 "nominal ground resolution of 40 feet"; KH-4B J-3 camera 5 feet. C-119 "Pelican 9", Capt Harold E. Mitchell.
- https://www.afhistory.org/august-19-1960/ : "Capt Harold E. Mitchell pilots a Fairchild C-119 to make the first aerial retrieval of an orbited capsule." Unit: 6593d Test Squadron (Special), Mackay Trophy.

Conflicts to handle in script: failure count (12 failures + XIII no camera vs CIA museum "first 13"); EO date (22 vs 24 Feb); coverage-area figures. Pick NRO phrasing for failures. Unfetched: nro.gov "The CORONA Story" PDF (fetch failed), NRO archived facts page (viewer shell only), DTIC CORONA book (not opened). Resolution of "more coverage than all U-2 missions combined": supported by Army and CIA monograph, not the CIA stories page.

## 2. Footage (all opened)

1. https://archive.org/details/1960-08-15_space_triumph : Universal newsreel "Space Triumph. Discoverer Capsule Recovered From Orbit", 3:37, 640x480 B&W with sound. Page marks Public Domain Mark. Caveat: newsreel is Universal-produced; the PD label is the archive's claim, not a verified copyright review (clearance risk, check before use). Content: Discoverer XIII capsule recovery near Hawaii, flag to Eisenhower; description notes "glitches". Also Echo I and X-15 segments (not CORONA, ~2 min usable).
2. https://archive.org/details/18044nasaorbitalrecoveryvwr : Lockheed Discoverer film, ~13:52, color, 853x480, uploaded by PeriscopeFilm. Content matches the story exactly (XIII prep/launch, C-119s, sea recovery, flag, XIV launch and aerial recovery). RIGHTS PROBLEM: page license is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 (Periscope's scan) and the film is a Lockheed contractor production; this is a commercial-use risk, not safe PD. Do not use without a license or an independent PD copy.
3. https://www.cia.gov/legacy/museum/artifact/corona-film : photo (film canister), no rights notice on page. US gov origin, likely PD, but unconfirmed.
4. https://www.nro.gov/About-NRO/history/history-corona/ lists photos ("In-air recovery of a CORONA canister", "Engineers Ready Corona"); no URLs obtained. Likely PD gov imagery; confirm before use.
5. https://www.usgs.gov/centers/eros/science/usgs-eros-archive-declassified-data-declassified-satellite-imagery-1 : declassified CORONA imagery data (search result only, page not opened). Good for Soviet-territory stills.
6. https://stock.periscopefilm.com/?p=319889 : commercial stock, not PD (search result only).
- DVIDS: searched; results were generic C-130 airdrop clips, none CORONA-related. Nothing usable verified (no modern aerial-capsule-recovery clip found).
- NARA catalog / NRO-specific archive.org CORONA films: none found. https://archive.org/details/nro-freq-scrape is NRO documents (not opened), not video.

Net moving footage: ~3.5 min PD-labelled (partly relevant, ~1.5 min of capsule recovery) + 13.9 min restricted. Verdict on ">=6 min PD moving footage reachable": NOT confirmed. Gap, would need NARA search (catalog.archives.gov, not reached), more archive.org searching, Air Force Historical Support Division, or licensing Periscope. Stills (CORONA imagery, diagrams, docs) are plentiful, so a doc-and-stills treatment with maps/animation is feasible; 15-20 min of mostly stills risks retention.
