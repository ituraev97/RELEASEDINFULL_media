# FILE 005 — Roswell, According to the Air Force · research notes

Rule: every claim → a URL actually opened in this run (2026-11-02). apps.dtic.mil, secretsdeclassified.af.mil and gao.gov are blocked for the build environment; the three primary reports were read from archive.org copies of the official prints (scan + OCR text downloaded and read).

## A. GAO report (primary)
GAO/NSIAD-95-187, "Government Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico," report to Rep. Steven H. Schiff, July 28, 1995 (B-262046).
- Copy read: https://archive.org/details/roswell-rapporto-gao (PDF 26 pp. + OCR). Official listing: https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad-95-187 (blocked here).
- p. 1: "On July 8, 1947, the Roswell Army Air Field (RAAF) public information office in Roswell, New Mexico, reported the crash and recovery of a 'flying disc.'" "The following day, the press reported that the Commanding General of the U.S. Eighth Air Force, Fort Worth, Texas, announced that RAAF personnel had recovered a crashed radar-tracking (weather) balloon, not a 'flying disc.'"
- p. 2: "RAAF administrative records (from Mar. 1945 through Dec. 1949) and RAAF outgoing messages (from Oct. 1946 through Dec. 1949) were destroyed. The document disposition form does not indicate what organization or person destroyed the records and when or under what authority". Search "yielded two records originating in 1947—a July 1947 history report by the combined 509th Bomb Group and RAAF and an FBI teletype message dated July 8, 1947." Blue Book Special Report No. 14 did not mention the crash.
- p. 4: 509th/RAAF history: the PIO "was kept quite busy . . . answering inquiries on the 'flying disc,' which was reported to be in [the] possession of the 509th Bomb Group. The object turned out to be a radar tracking balloon." Chief Archivist: "many of the Air Force organizational records covering this time period were destroyed without entering a citation for the governing disposition authority."
- p. 4–5: FBI teletype July 8, 1947, Dallas → HQ and Cincinnati: Eighth Air Force official "telephonically informed" FBI Dallas of the recovery "of a hexagonal-shaped disc suspended from a large balloon by cable"; disc and balloon being sent to Wright Field; "the recovered object resembled a high-altitude weather balloon with a radar reflector"; "no further investigation by the FBI was being conducted." Teletype facsimile: app. II (p. 16). 509th history excerpt: app. I (pp. 14–15).

## B. USAF, "The Roswell Report: Fact vs. Fiction in the New Mexico Desert" (1995) — contains the July 1994 Weaver report and attachments (primary)
- Copy read: https://archive.org/details/DTIC_ADA326148 (DTIC ADA326148; PDF 881 pp. + OCR). Official listing in topics.md: https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/html/tr/ADA326148/index.html (blocked here).
- Introduction (McAndrew): secrecy oaths — "if active or former Air Force officials had been sworn to a secrecy oath, they were to be freed from it"; Mogul "had been declassified for more than twenty years — a shadowy, formerly Top Secret project, code-named MOGUL"; objective "to develop a long-range system capable of detecting Soviet nuclear detonations and ballistic missile launches"; balloon trains "over thirty research balloons and experimental sensors strung together and stretching more than 600 feet".
- Weaver memo to SECAF, JUL 27 1994 (pdf p. 19): "The Air Force efforts did not identify any indication that the 'Roswell Incident' was any type of extraterrestrial event or that the Air Force has engaged in a 47 year conspiracy or 'cover-up' of information relating to it. Therefore, it is assumed that pro-UFO groups will strongly object to the attached report and denounce it as either shortsighted or a continuation of the 'cover-up' conspiracy."
- Report (pdf pp. 21–42): "Research revealed that the 'Roswell Incident' was not even considered a UFO event until the 1978-1980 time frame"; July 8 Roswell Daily Record "RAAF Captures Flying Saucer On Ranch In Roswell Region"; July 9: Ramey said the debris "was determined to be a weather balloon"; Brazel and son Vernon found material June 14, 1947 ("a large area of bright wreckage made up of rubber strips, tinfoil, a rather tough paper, and sticks"); Brazel went to Roswell July 7 and contacted the Sheriff; Marcel and "a man in plain clothes" accompanied him home; full RDR quote incl. "about 200 yards in diameter", "maybe five pounds", "no sign of any metal in the area which might have been used for an engine and no sign of any propellers", "tape with flowers printed upon it", "some eyelets in the paper".
- 1978 National Enquirer article reporting Marcel's claim; Stanton Friedman met Marcel and began investigating; Moore & Berlitz, The Roswell Incident (1980); witness accounts "related their stories in their older years"; growth "from a minimal amount of debris recovered from a small area to airplane loads of debris from multiple huge 'debris fields'"; "sticks, paper, tape and tinfoil has since grown to exotic metals"; "Most versions now claim that there were two crash sites"; "Most of the Roswell stories that contain the recovery of ... bodies also show them being shipped to Wright Field."
- Roswell "is not referred to in the official USAF investigation of UFOs reported in Project Bluebook".
- Search "not limited to unclassified materials, but also would include records of the highest classification and compartmentation".
- "An Extraterrestrial Craft: The Air Force research found absolutely no indication that what happened near Roswell in 1947, involved any type of extraterrestrial spacecraft." Executive summary: "Air Force research efforts did not disclose any records of the recovery of any 'alien' bodies or extraterrestrial materials."
- Mogul (pdf p. 38–39): 1946 HQ AMC memo specified data classified "Top Secret Priority 1A"; long-range balloon-borne low-frequency acoustic detection proposed by Dr. Maurice Ewing; "Because the Soviet Union's borders were closed"; NYU team at Alamogordo AAF; "service flights" not logged; gaps for Flights 2–4 and 9; radar targets: "Some of the early developmental radar targets were manufactured by a toy or novelty company ... aluminum 'foil' or foil-backed paper, balsa wood beams that were coated in an 'Elmer's-type' glue ... acetate and/or cloth reinforcing tape, single strand and braided nylon twine, brass eyelets"; "purplish-pink tape with symbols on it"; Moore "opined that the material was most likely the shredded remains of a multi-neoprene balloon train with multiple radar reflectors" and "most probably from Flight 4"; Crary journal: "Flight 4 was launched on June 4, 1947, but was not recovered by the NYU group"; "ultimately found by the rancher, Brazel, ten days later".
- Cavitt (pdf p. 39 and sworn statement p. 130, May 24, 1994): CIC special agent; "resemble bamboo type square sticks one quarter to one half inch square, that were very light, as well as some sort of metallic reflecting material that was also very light .... I remember recognizing this material as being consistent with a weather balloon." Report: "the last living member of the three persons officially acknowledged to have recovered material from the Foster Ranch."
- "Concerning the initial announcement, 'RAAF Captures Flying Disc,' research failed to locate any documented evidence as to why that statement was made."
- Trakowski: radar targets "fabricated by toy or novelty companies using a purplish-pink tape with flower and heart symbols on it."
- Pflock (Roswell in Perspective, 1994) concluded the Brazel debris "was probably debris from a MOGUL balloon".
- Conclusion (pdf p. 42): "the most likely source of the wreckage recovered from the Brazel Ranch was from one of the Project Mogul balloon trains"; "no specific indication found to indicate an official preplanned cover story"; "overreaction by Colonel Blanchard and Major Marcel in originally reporting that a 'flying disc' had been recovered when, at that time, nobody knew for sure what that term even meant, since it had only been in use for a couple of weeks."
- Attachment 1, Washington Post, Jan. 14, 1994 (pdf p. 23ff): Schiff wrote Les Aspin "last March" [1993]; "there are indications from the runaround that I got that whatever it was, it wasn't a balloon. Apparently, it's another government coverup."
- Attachment 19 (pdf p. 164): Air Materiel Command letter, Wright Field, 8 July 1946, on project "Mogul" classification ("TOP SECRET"). Attachment 21 (pdf p. 172): Moore sworn statement, June 8, 1994 ("In the early flights at Alamogordo, starting in June, 1947"). Attachment 31 (pdf p. 264): photos of ML-307C/AP device with vintage neoprene balloon and debris.

## C. USAF, "The Roswell Report: Case Closed" (McAndrew, 1997) (primary)
- Copy read: https://archive.org/details/DTIC_ADA326147 (DTIC ADA326147; PDF 232 pp. + OCR); same print at https://archive.org/details/AFD-101027-030. Official: https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA326147.pdf (blocked here).
- Foreword, Sheila E. Widnall: "complete and open explanation of the events".
- pp. 1–3: "the Air Force operations that inspired reports of 'bodies' ... did not occur in 1947"; conclusions: "Air Force activities which occurred over a period of many years have been consolidated and are now represented to have occurred in two or three days in July 1947." "'Aliens' observed in the New Mexico desert were probably anthropomorphic test dummies"; recovery units "always seemed to arrive shortly after the crash ... were actually accurate descriptions of Air Force personnel engaged in anthropomorphic dummy recovery operations"; hospital claims "a combination of two separate incidents: 1) a 1956 KC-97 aircraft accident in which 11 Air Force members lost their lives; and, 2) a 1959 manned balloon mishap in which two Air Force pilots were injured."
- p. 5: accounts collected "as much as 40 years after the alleged incident"; Fig. 3: Mogul balloon train "similar to one found on a ranch 75 miles northwest of Roswell, N.M. in June 1947".
- p. 23: "From 1953 to 1959, anthropomorphic dummies were used by the U.S. Air Force Aero Medical Laboratory as part of the high altitude aircraft escape projects High Dive and Excelsior"; "altitudes up to 98,000 feet"; "Forty-three high altitude balloon flights carrying 67 anthropomorphic dummies were launched and recovered throughout New Mexico between June 1954 and February 1959"; "the majority of dummies impacted outside the confines of military reservations in eastern New Mexico, near Roswell, and in areas surrounding the Tularosa Valley". Fig. 23: High Dive dummy launch, White Sands Proving Ground, June 11, 1957 (USAF photo). Fig. 29: High Dive test, Oct. 8, 1957, White Sands (USAF photo). p. 30: recovery crews of the Holloman AFB Balloon Branch, vehicles and aircraft (USAF photos).
- Footnote p. 1: Mogul records declassified under Executive Order 11652 (March 6, 1972).
