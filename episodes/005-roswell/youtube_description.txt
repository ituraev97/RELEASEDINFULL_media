On July 8, 1947, Roswell Army Air Field announced it had recovered a "flying disc." Within a day, the Army said it was a radar-tracking balloon. Decades later, under pressure from Congress, the U.S. Air Force searched its own records and published two official reports: a 1994 report naming the then-Top Secret balloon project Mogul as the most likely source of the debris, and the 1997 report "Case Closed," which said accounts of bodies came from 1950s test-dummy drops and other events misdated to 1947. We read the documents — and the objections.

Facts come from documents and official sources. Claims are attributed. Theories are labelled as theories.
