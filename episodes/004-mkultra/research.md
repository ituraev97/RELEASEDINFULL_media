## A. Senate joint hearing, Aug. 3, 1977 (primary)
"Project MKULTRA, the CIA's Program of Research in Behavioral Modification." Joint Hearing before the Select Committee on Intelligence and the Subcommittee on Health and Scientific Research of the Committee on Human Resources, U.S. Senate, 95th Cong., 1st sess., Aug. 3, 1977.
- Official: https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/sites/default/files/hearings/95mkultra.pdf (listed in topics.md; blocked here)
- Scan used (archive.org): https://archive.org/details/project-mkultra-the-cias-program-of-research-in-behavioral-modification_202305 — PDF + OCR text downloaded; same text also at https://archive.org/details/jointhearing_project-mkultra_1977 and https://archive.org/details/projectmkultraci0000unit
- PDF page numbers below = page index of the archive.org PDF (173 pp.).

Senator Kennedy, opening statement (pdf pp. 6–8):
- "The Central Intelligence Agency drugged American citizens without their knowledge or consent."
- "The records of all these activities were destroyed in January 1973, at the instruction of then CIA Director Richard Helms."
- "one individual, through a Freedom of Information Request, accomplished what two U.S. Senate committees could not"; records discovered in March, Congress told in July.

Adm. Stansfield Turner (DCI), prepared statement (pdf pp. 8–12):
- letter of July 15, 1977; "discovery of seven boxes of documents related to Project MKULTRA, a closely held CIA project conducted from 1953-1964"; "umbrella project".
- Olson: "In 1953 Dr. Olson, a civilian employee of the Army at Fort Detrick, leaped to his death from a hotel room window in New York City about a week after having unwittingly consumed LSD"; the cryptonym became public in 1975 during the Rockefeller Commission and Church Committee investigations "when details of the drug-related death of Dr. Frank Olson were publicized".
- 1963 IG report quoted: "Present practice is to maintain no records of the planning and approval of test programs."
- Found at the Retired Records Center outside Washington by an employee responsible for FOIA requests; sent there in 1970 by the Budget and Fiscal Section; "The reason for this departure from normal procedure is not known. As a result of it, however, the material escaped retrieval and destruction in 1973 by the then-retiring Director of the Office as well as discovery in 1975".
- "the recovered folders are finance folders. The bulk of the material in them consists of approvals for advance of funds, vouchers, accountings, and the like"; does not include status reports.
- 149 MKULTRA subprojects; two boxes of miscellaneous papers; 33 additional unrelated subprojects; 15 categories (incl. 6 subprojects involving tests on unwitting subjects).
- "names of 185 non-government researchers and assistants"; "80 institutions"; "44 colleges or universities, 15 research foundations or chemical or pharmaceutical companies and the like, 12 hospitals or clinics (in addition to those associated with universities), and 3 penal institutions".
- $375,000 to the building fund of a private medical institution via an intermediary; "made it appear to be a private donation"; "matched by federal funds".
- "some of the unwitting drug testing was carried on in safehouses in San Francisco and New York City".
- "Most of the people and institutions involved are not aware of Agency sponsorship."
- Church Report p. 392: "According to the CIA, the project was decreased significantly each budget year until its complete termination in the late 1960s" (script attributes the 1964 end date to the CIA).
- Q&A (pdf p. 22–23): requests started with "several Freedom of Information Act requests"; Mr. Laubinger searched "through every file with which the branch that conducted this type of activity had any conceivable connection"; "Very late in March, he discovered these seven boxes."
- Appendix B, CIA memo of June 22, 1977 (pdf p. 106): "As a result of John Marks FOIA request (F-76-374), all of the MKULTRA material in OTS possession was reviewed ... the OTS material in the Retired Records Center was searched."

Church Committee Final Report, Book I (1976), pp. 385–422, reprinted as Appendix A (pdf pp. 69–102). Official copy: https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/sites-default-files-94755-i.pdf (blocked here; text read in the hearing reprint):
- p. 389–390 (pdf 72–73): "In January 1973, MKULTRA records were destroyed by Technical Services Division personnel acting on the verbal orders of Dr. Sidney Gottlieb, Chief of TSD. Dr. Gottlieb has testified, and former Director Helms has confirmed, that in ordering the records destroyed, Dr. Gottlieb was carrying out the verbal order of then DCI Helms." MKULTRA began with a proposal from ADDP Richard Helms to the DCI; quoted proposal: "Research to develop a capability in the covert use of biological and chemical materials." (fn: Memorandum from ADDP Helms to DCI Dulles, 4/3/53). "MKULTRA was approved by the DCI on April 13, 1953". IG 1963 reasons for sensitivity.
- p. 390–391 (pdf 73–74): three phases ("first, the search for materials suitable for study; second, laboratory testing on voluntary human subjects in various types of institutions; third, the application of MKULTRA materials in normal life settings"); "standing arrangements with specialists in universities, pharmaceutical houses, hospitals, state and federal institutions, and private research organizations"; grants "under ostensible research foundation auspices, thereby concealing the CIA's interest from the specialist's institution"; NIMH Addiction Research Center, Lexington, Ky., "a prison for drug addicts serving sentences for drug violations"; "volunteer prisoners"; "As a reward for participation in the program, the addicts were provided with the drug of their addiction."; "LSD was one of the materials tested"; "surreptitious administration to unwitting nonvolunteer subjects in normal life settings by undercover officers of the Bureau of Narcotics acting for the CIA"; rationale "that testing of materials under accepted scientific procedures fails to disclose the full pattern of reactions and attributions that may occur in operational situations"; "the effectiveness of the substances on individuals at all social levels, high and low, native American and foreign, is of great significance".
- p. 394–397 (pdf 77–79), Olson: died Nov. 27, 1953; "unwittingly received approximately 70 micrograms of LSD in a glass of Cointreau he drank on November 19, 1953"; drug placed by CIA officer Dr. Robert Lashbrook; Nov. 18, 1953, "a group of ten scientists from the CIA and Camp Detrick attended a semi-annual review and analysis conference at a cabin located at Deep Creek Lake, Maryland"; "an expert in aerobiology"; "Neither the Chief of TSS nor the DDP specifically authorized the experiment"; "About twenty minutes after they finished their Cointreau, Gottlieb informed the other participants that they had received LSD" (per Gottlieb); symptoms of paranoia; psychiatric help in New York from Dr. Harold Abramson "whose research on LSD had been funded indirectly by the CIA"; "fell to his death from a tenth story window in the Statler Hotel"; detailed account p. 397 (pdf 80): "At approximately 2:30 a.m. Saturday, November 28" (summary on p. 394 says he "died on November 27, 1953"; Ford signing statement and S. 3035 file give Nov. 28 — script uses Nov. 28); CIA "made a substantial effort to ensure that his family received death benefits, but did not notify the Olsons of the circumstances surrounding his demise."
- p. 401–402 (pdf 84): unwitting testing "finally halted in 1963" after the IG survey of TSD; May 24, 1963, DDP said "the Director indicated no disagreement and therefore the 'testing' will continue"; IG recommended termination; "The project was suspended following the Inspector General's report"; Dec. 17, 1963 Helms memo: "the only realistic method of maintaining the capability, considering the intended operational use of materials to influence human behavior as the operational targets will certainly be unwitting"; "pro forma" ... "false sense of accomplishment and readiness". IG 1963 quoted: subjects "seldom accessible beyond the first hours of the test"; "the test subject has become ill for hours or days".
- p. 403–404 (pdf 86–87): Helms testimony 9/11/75: "...he thought it would be a good idea if these files were destroyed ... so that anybody who assisted us in the past would not be subject to follow-up or questions, embarrassment, if you will."; waiver of CSI 70-10 ("retirement of inactive records"; Karamessines: "The heart of this judgment is to ensure that the complete story can be reconstructed in later years and by people who may be unfamiliar with the events."); destruction made it impossible to determine the full range; "prevented the CIA from locating and providing medical assistance to the individuals who were subjects in the program"; fn: "at least one assistant to Dr. Gottlieb protested against the document destruction"; IG 1963 quoted: "Files are notably incomplete, poorly organized, and lacking in evaluative statements"; "A substantial portion of the MKULTRA record appears to rest in the memories of the principal officers".
- Helms proposal: six percent of TSD R&D budget "without the establishment of formal contractual relations" because contracts would reveal government interest; qualified individuals "are most reluctant to enter into signed agreements of any sort which connect them with this activity" (pdf 87).

## B. CIA Inspector General, "Report of Inspection of MKULTRA," July 26, 1963 (primary)
CIA doc C06767515, approved for release 2019/04/03. archive.org copy: https://archive.org/details/cia-readingroom-document-06767515 (PDF 46 pp. + OCR downloaded). NSArchive copy listed in topics.md (blocked here).
- p. 2: "MKULTRA was authorized by the then Director of Central Intelligence, Mr. Allen W. Dulles, in 1953"; "Normal procedures for project approval, funding, and accounting were waived."; "The concepts involved in manipulating human behavior are found by many people both within and outside the Agency to be distasteful and unethical."
- p. 16 (report p. 14): "Present practice is to maintain no records of the planning and approval of test programs."; IG recommends "termination of this phase of the MKULTRA program".
- Report para. 30: "TSD has initiated 144 projects relating to the control of human behavior ... during the ten years of operation of the MKULTRA program."
- p. 38 (annex): DCI memo to Deputy Director (Administration), stamped APR 13 1953, "Project MKULTRA – Extremely Sensitive Research and Development Program": "I have, therefore, approved the obligation and expenditure by TSS of 6% of the total budget ... without the signing of the usual contracts or other written agreements." "This 6% will be handled as Project MKULTRA." (signed Allen W. Dulles, p. 39). p. 40: memo for the file, 21 April 1953: MKULTRA established by memo "dated 3 April 1953 from DD/P to DCI as a result of which on 13 April 1953, DCI wrote to DD/A approving the project".
