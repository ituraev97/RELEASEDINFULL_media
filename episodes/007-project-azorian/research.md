## A. CIA, "Project AZORIAN: The Story of the Hughes Glomar Explorer," *Studies in Intelligence* (Fall 1985, per NSArchive), SECRET NOFORN, excised; stamp "APPROVED FOR RELEASE DATE: 01-04-2010"
- Copy read: https://archive.org/details/cia-readingroom-document-0005301269 (CIA Reading Room 0005301269; 50 pp.). Local: /home/claude/src/docs/azorian.pdf, azorian.txt (OCR), az_clean.txt; page renders /home/claude/src/docs/hi/.
- Cover note (pdf p.1): "almost all aspects of the Hughes Glomar Explorer project are still classified"; court-acknowledged facts: CIA sponsorship "for intelligence collection purposes", Hughes Tool Co., Summa Corp., Global Marine; senior CIA officials in 1975 tried "to persuade members of the media not to broadcast or publish". Policy still prohibits: "the specific purpose of the AZORIAN mission; the degree of success; operational details; … project funding matters."
- p.1: "In March 1968 a Soviet submarine of the G-II class was lost with all hands, 16,500 feet below the surface of the Pacific Ocean. On 8 August 1974, [redacted] that submarine was brought to the surface in [redacted] a recovery system designed and developed specifically for that mission."
- p.2: Jack Anderson disclosed the project in March 1975 on national TV and radio; "original press leak had occurred in the Los Angeles Times in February 1975. The Times story was unspecific, and wrong in important facts". Leak followed "a break-in and robbery in June 1974 at Summa Corporation headquarters in Los Angeles"; feared stolen documents might include "a memorandum from a senior Hughes official to Howard Hughes describing a proposed CIA attempt to recover a sunken Soviet submarine". "code-named AZORIAN, not 'JENNIFER' as stated in the press".
- p.2–3 Project Origin: "diesel-powered Soviet G-II-class ballistic missile submarine pendant 722 sailed from Petropavlovsk on about 1 March 1968 to take a patrol station northeast of Hawaii … where it would be available for nuclear attack on U.S. targets in event of war. The submarine suffered an accident—cause unknown—and sank 1,560 miles northwest of Hawaii." Soviets: "massive two-month search effort … The Soviet search was fruitless". The history calls it "the G-722" (e.g. "recovering components of the G-722").
- Organizing: letter from Deputy SecDef David Packard to DCI Richard Helms, 1 April 1969, asking for a study and for "CIA to take the lead". Security system "code-named JENNIFER". Special Projects Staff, John Parangosky.
- Early concepts: "Brute Force" direct lift (Rosenberg Winch, "massive floating winches"), trade ballast/buoyancy ("buoyant material would be carried to the bottom"), at-depth generation of buoyancy (gas). Heavy-lift concept favored by July 1970.
- 30 Oct 1970 ExCom: "estimated 1,750-ton target object from the 16,500-foot depth … large (565' by 106') surface ship"; deep-sea mining cover story; "estimated the probability of success at about 10 percent … Just prior to the mission believed the probability of success to be about 90 percent."
- Costs: all dollar figures redacted ("Total cost now was projected [redacted]"; "total project cost had grown by 66 percent to [redacted]").
- 28 Aug 1972: Adm. Moorer, Chairman JCS, "could not support the proposed AZORIAN mission". 18 Sept 1972: Rush (Packard's successor as DepSecDef) "estimated the chances of technical success at 20 to 30 percent". 11 Dec 1972: "the final decision, made by the President, was to continue the AZORIAN project".
- April 1971 Global Marine press release: "would build a 600-foot mining ship for the Hughes Tool Company (HTC)". "On 4 November 1972, the Hughes Glomar Explorer was launched with the usual champagne christening ceremony and speeches" (Sun Shipbuilding, Chester, Pa.).
- Ship: derrick "236 feet above the waterline"; "unusually large well area"; well gates. "116-foot beam was too wide to permit passage through the Panama Canal" → transit around South America via Strait of Magellan (12,745 nm, 50 days). Valparaíso during the 11 Sept 1973 coup: "a bizarre coincidence".
- Crew: "a total of 178 was decided upon, the maximum size limited by lifeboat capacity."
- USIB, May 1974 (quoted): "Acquisition of the nuclear warheads and the SS-N-5 missile system, together with related documents, would provide a much-improved baseline for estimates of the current and future Soviet strategic threat."
- "President Nixon approved the mission on 7 June, with the proviso that actual recovery must not be undertaken before his return from an impending 27 June–3 July visit to the Soviet Union."
- 20 June 1974: moved from anchorage off Long Beach; set course for the recovery site. Arrived 4 July 1974 at 1301 local.
- Weather: typhoon "Gilda" swells 12 July; cracks found 14 July "in both the forward port and after starboard docking leg guide structures"; tropical storm "Harriet".
- Bel Hudson (British merchant ship), medical assistance; HGE surgeon treated the seaman; "The HGE responded that it was engaged in deep-ocean mining testing using a prototype mining machine. It was hoped the Soviets were monitoring this exchange."
- Chazhma (pp. 38–40): "Missile Range Instrumentation Ship Chazhma", arrived 18 July; helicopter "made many approaches … taking photographs from all angles"; "piles of canvas-covered crates be placed on the HGE's helicopter deck"; "be prepared to order emergency destruction of sensitive material … if the Soviets attempted to board the ship … alerted, but guns were not issued." Radio: "We are on our way home and heard your fog horn. What are you doing here?" / "We are conducting ocean mining tests—deep-ocean mining tests." / "What kind of vessel are you?" "A deep-ocean mining vessel." / "How much time will you be here?" "We expect to finish testing in two to three weeks." / "I wish you all the best."
- SB-10 Soviet salvage tug arrived 22 July; "closing to a distance of 75 yards"; "close surveillance … had lasted 13 days and 16 hours".
- Pipe: "15-ton pipe double"; doubles of 60 ft (30 doubles = 1,800 ft). "Lift-off — On 1 August, [redacted] lifted from the bottom!" "Two hundred and seventy-four doubles or 16,440 feet of pipe had been deployed and were coming up" (2 August).
- Cover radio 3 August: "believed the 'nodule collector vehicle' might have collided with a hard silt-covered outcrop. It was this fictional 'casualty' … excuse … to enter Midway for repairs".
- "One can only conjecture the reaction and chagrin of Soviet authorities when they later realized that two Soviet Navy ships were on the scene and, in effect, witnessed the recovery operation against their lost submarine."
- p.46: "Evidence of plutonium was found … apparently primarily from one or more of the nuclear torpedoes whose high explosive had detonated without creating a nuclear explosion—the war heads were 'one point safe.'"
- Recovery phase finished 9 August; "Event 36-A". Lahaina Roads, Maui, 16 Aug.
- p.49 (Packard): "if they were to wait until all the risks were eliminated, the project would never get under way."
- Several pages fully withheld (e.g. pdf p.3, 7–9), large redaction boxes (p.11 etc.).

## F. Phillippi v. CIA, 546 F.2d 1009 (D.C. Cir. 1976)
- https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/546/1009/204926/ — FOIA request for records on CIA efforts to persuade media not to publish about the Glomar Explorer; Agency: "in the interest of national security, involvement by the U.S. Government in the activities which are the subject matter of your request can neither be confirmed nor denied."
