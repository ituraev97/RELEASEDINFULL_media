## A. The CIA U-2 history (primary)
Gregory W. Pedlow & Donald E. Welzenbach, *The CIA and the U-2 Program, 1954–1974*, CIA History Staff / Center for the Study of Intelligence. Written 1992 (S/NOFORN); redacted version published 1998; less-redacted version released Aug. 15, 2013 to the National Security Archive under FOIA.

- **NSArchive EBB 434 (Aug. 15, 2013), ed. Jeffrey T. Richelson**: https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB434/ — "obtained under the Freedom of Information Act by National Security Archive senior fellow Jeffrey T. Richelson"; "2005 Freedom of Information Act request"; "a heavily redacted version of the U-2 portion was published, in 1998"; "Written 1992 (S/NOFORN)"; new material: "Numerous references to Area 51 and Groom Lake, with a map of the area"; Pocock's notes cite AEC tests forcing evacuation of Groom Lake (p. 133).
- **2013 release, Chapter 2 (PDF)**: https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB434/docs/U2%20-%20Chapter%202.pdf (read via web reader; OCR)
  - p. 56: 12 April 1955 search flight by Bissell, Ritland, Johnson, LeVier; "They spotted what appeared to be an airstrip by a salt flat known as Groom Lake, near the northeast corner of the Atomic Energy Commission's (AEC) Nevada Proving Ground." LeVier "set the plane down on the lakebed".
  - p. 57: "Kelly Johnson called it the Paradise Ranch, which was soon shortened to the Ranch"; "the base acquired yet another unofficial name, Watertown Strip"; base ready by July 1955 (reader summary).
  - pp. 68–70: accidental lift-off during taxi trials ("I had no intentions whatsoever of flying" — LeVier); first planned flight 4 August 1955; "official" first flight 8 August 1955 with Bissell, Ritland present; "Kelly Johnson had met his eight-month deadline"; 16 Aug 52,000 ft; 8 Sept 65,600 ft; "altitudes never before attained in sustained flight".
  - p. 72: Monday/Friday flights from Burbank; MATS C-54 from 3 Oct 1955; "Bissell's Narrow-Gauge Airline" (James Cunningham); "a MATS aircraft bound for Area 51 crashed on 17 November", 14 killed, "the greatest single loss of life in the entire U-2 program".
  - pp. 72–73: "U-2s, UFOs, and Operation BLUE BOOK" (text identical in the 1998 scan, see B).
- **Quoted 2013 text on the Area 51 name (press reproducing the history)**: Las Vegas Sun, Aug. 15, 2013: https://lasvegassun.com/news/2013/aug/15/government-acknowledges-area-51-declassified-spy-p/ — "President Eisenhower also approved the addition of this strip of wasteland, known by its map designation as Area 51, to the Nevada Test Site"; group "asked the AEC to add it to its real estate holdings in Nevada"; "To make the new facility in the middle of nowhere sound more attractive to his workers, Kelly Johnson called it the Paradise Ranch, which was soon shortened to the Ranch."
- Foreign Policy, Aug. 15, 2013: https://foreignpolicy.com/2013/08/15/declassified-the-cias-secret-history-of-area-51/ — quotes: strip "fashioned from compacted earth that had turned into ankle-deep dust"; "If LeVier had attempted to land on the airstrip, the plane would probably have nosed over when the wheels sank into the loose soil, killing or injuring all of the key figures in the U-2 project."; nuclear tests "whose fallout was expected to contaminate the Groom Lake facility"; history = 355 pages.
- **Chapter 1**: https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB434/docs/U2%20-%20Chapter%201.pdf — Eisenhower approval Nov. 1954 (Land/Killian meeting early Nov.; Goodpaster memo of conference 24 Nov. 1954); Bissell DCI special assistant; Johnson promised flying by August 1955, about eight months after construction began.
- **Chapter 3**: https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB434/docs/U2%20-%20Chapter%203.pdf — first overflight of USSR Wed. 4 July 1956 (Article 347, pilot Stockman); Bissell: first flights overflew "both Moscow and Leningrad" (p. 105); Soviets detected but "could not track U-2s consistently"; photos showed Soviet fighters climbing and falling away (p. 108); 10 July 1956 Soviet note: "twin-engine medium bomber" (p. 109); design altitude ~72,000 ft beyond known Soviet weapons (p. 93).
- **Chapter 4**: https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB434/docs/U2%20-%20Chapter%204.pdf — Powers 1 May 1960, "brought down by a near miss of a Soviet surface-to-air missile", "Sverdlovsk SA-2 battery" (p. 177); "President Eisenhower's order ending overflights of the Soviet Union" (p. 181); "the U-2 and its two proposed successors, the OXCART aircraft and the reconnaissance satellite" (p. 191).

## C. Haines (primary, CIA)
- Gerald K. Haines, "CIA's Role in the Study of UFOs, 1947–90," *Studies in Intelligence* (unclassified edition 1997), CIA doc C00242525. archive.org copy: https://archive.org/download/CIAUFO/estPubDateCiasRoleInTheStudyOfUfos1947-90ByGeraldK.HainesDoc_0005517742_text.pdf (CIA page: https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/studies-in-intelligence/archives/vol-40-no-5/the-cias-role-in-the-study-of-ufos-1947-90/ — listed in search, blocked here). Page 73 read visually:
  - "According to later estimates from CIA officials who worked on the U-2 project and the OXCART (SR-71, or Blackbird) project, over half of all UFO reports from the late 1950s through the 1960s were accounted for by manned reconnaissance flights (namely the U-2) over the United States. This led the Air Force to make misleading and deceptive statements to the public in order to allay public fears and to protect an extraordinarily sensitive national security project. While perhaps justified, this deception added fuel to the later conspiracy theories and the coverup controversy of the 1970s. The percentage of what the Air Force considered unexplained UFO sightings fell to 5.9 percent in 1955 and to 4 percent in 1956."
  - "The early U-2s were silver (they were later painted black)"; USSR atomic bomb 1949, hydrogen bomb Aug. 1953; Nov. 1954 CIA U-2 project with Lockheed's Skunk Works in Burbank and Kelly Johnson.
  - Note: Haines writes "OXCART (SR-71…)"; per Robarge, OXCART was the CIA A-12; the SR-71 was the Air Force variant. Script says "OXCART" and "A-12".

## D. OXCART / A-12 (primary, CIA)
- David Robarge, *Archangel: CIA's Supersonic A-12 Reconnaissance Aircraft*, 2nd ed., Jan. 2012, CSI: https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/b45f5f8f5e4937963d9e9931313d84a4/Archangel-CIAs-Supersonic-A-12-Reconnaissance-Aircraft.pdf (read via web reader)
  - p. 11: "Mach 3.2, or 2,150 miles per hour" at "a mid-range altitude of 91,000 feet"; "build over 90 percent of the A-12's airframe out of a titanium alloy".
  - pp. 16–17: unofficial first flight 25 April 1962, Lou Schalk; official first flight 30 April 1962, at "the Nevada facility"/"the test site".
  - p. 22: CIA and Air Force "had ordered" 15 OXCART aircraft.
  - p. 27: White House announcement 29 Feb. 1964: "The A-11 aircraft now at Edwards Air Force Base are undergoing extensive tests"; the YF-12A interceptor was what was surfaced as the "A-11".
  - pp. 34–35: BLACK SHIELD, first operational mission 31 May 1967 over North Vietnam (from Kadena, Okinawa).
  - pp. 37–38: after USS Pueblo seized 23 Jan. 1968, Jack Weeks' A-12 mission 26 Jan.; "the only time an A-12 camera took an image of the Pueblo".
  - pp. 37–41: last operational A-12 flight May 1968 (caption: Article 127, 6 May 1968); SR-71 = "the Air Force's OXCART reconnaissance variant".
